What Nikita Bier Stepping Down Reveals About X Corp’s Weaknesses

2026-08-11 6 min read # Tech # Elon Musk # Thought

To be honest, if I were Nikita Bier, I would last no more than six months at X Corp before stepping back into an advisor role. The pressure is simply too intense, and the job is fundamentally incompatible with what he is actually good at.

He is more of an advisor/consultant/growth person: someone who studies mechanisms, stimulates interaction, creates virality, adjusts the timeline—in plain terms, someone better suited to setting a "strategic direction" than standing on the edge of a volcano every day like Elon Musk, posting on X nonstop as if he were customer support. I see his X posts in my feed every day, and he gives me the impression of an on-call customer service representative who also has to absorb the enormous resentment caused by X's legacy problems. The pressure must be tremendous.

X's problems are fundamentally not just about product growth. In other words, Elon may have misunderstood the problem from the beginning, or perhaps his ambitions were simply too broad for one person like Nikita to handle. Advertising, political content in many different languages, pornographic content, other gray-area content, bots, shadow banning, and Grok being tied to the X platform—these are all problems, and most of them are inherited from the Twitter era. I registered my first X account in 2020, almost six years ago, and these problems already existed then. It has also been roughly three years since Elon acquired Twitter, hasn't it? Yet even X's most basic UI design still has not been properly sorted out; it looks like little more than Twitter with a different color scheme. In other words, the deeper problems cannot possibly be solved by one person in a single year.

So Nikita has to think about product direction, execute on it, maintain interaction and explain things on X, and respond to users' resentment at the same time. That is exhausting.

Why can't everyone post on X as frequently as Elon does? Because, fundamentally, he is not simply running an account—he is setting the tone of his own platform as the owner of X Corp. X itself is his political amplifier and public-opinion machine. If he stops posting, the platform's narrative starts to disperse. Of course, what he posts is very clearly mostly aligned with his own interests. But at the very least, X has a central narrative. Posting about Grok or "Scam Altman," for example, is a way of steering the narrative—telling users what X is talking about right now and which topics are hot. One of Elon's biggest jobs may well be posting on X, but other employees are in a completely different position.

So this only shows that X's problems cannot be solved simply by finding one smart advisor who knows product. Product debt, the scale of the platform, staffing, the owner's personal narrative, AI, payments, advertising, and bots are all tangled together, while the company's direction keeps becoming more diffuse. From what I see on X, the visible X team seems to consist mainly of Elon, Nikita, and Benji. In other words, there are very few people publicly articulating X's product narrative. X Corp obviously must have many more people behind the scenes, but only a handful seem willing or able to appear publicly, explain the direction, absorb users' anger, and set the pace. Benji is more of a design-execution person, and although I have seen some of the others, they are generally very low-profile. Naturally, users end up directing their dissatisfaction toward the two most visible figures. X Corp probably does not lack smart people; rather, its visible decision-making and product-narrative layer is too thin, and the amount of manpower at that layer is completely mismatched with the scale of the problems.

There is one more point I especially want to mention: why has Elon owned Twitter for so long without ever really fixing X? Because its ambitions keep expanding. One moment it is an everything app, the next it is a front end for Grok, and then it starts building X Money. It is hard to tell where the actual priority lies. As the owner, Elon can turn this chaos into a sense of corporate direction through his posts (and in fact, that is very much his style), but X Corp is still, at its core, a social media company. It still requires a huge amount of boring but necessary day-to-day governance. It does not even have basic customer support, and there does not appear to be much moderation either. (Of course, introducing moderation might itself become a political issue, but at the very least moderators could filter out NSFW material, gray-market content, and pornography.)

So Nikita stepping back into an advisor role actually makes a lot of sense.
